EXTERNAL INDUSTRY REFERENCE — U.S. CHEMICAL SAFETY BOARD (CSB) INVESTIGATION
Source: U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board, csb.gov
Facility: Honeywell, Geismar, Louisiana, USA
Incident date: 23 January 2023
Report status: Final report released 27 May 2025 (covers the January 2023
event and a related 7 June 2024 hydrofluoric acid release)

SUMMARY
A heat exchanger ruptured at the Honeywell Geismar facility, releasing
hydrogen fluoride (HF) gas, chlorine gas, and other process fluids to the
surrounding area.

INVESTIGATION SCOPE
The CSB's investigation examined the facility's process safety management
systems, including management of change, mechanical integrity, quality
assurance, pre-startup safety reviews, operating procedures, and contractor
management, as contributing factors.

KEY RECOMMENDATIONS (CSB, public record)
1. Honeywell should commission a comprehensive third-party audit of the
   facility's process safety and management systems against applicable
   federal standards.
2. Honeywell should require periodic reporting on the closure of audit
   findings until all are resolved.
3. Honeywell should perform a Safer Technologies and Alternatives Analysis
   for the affected production unit.
4. The U.S. EPA should prioritise hydrogen fluoride for risk evaluation
   under the Toxic Substances Control Act.

RELEVANCE TO HEAT-EXCHANGER-BEARING ASSETS
This is external, publicly documented industry experience — not an event at
this plant — included to demonstrate the kind of cross-industry lessons a
Lessons-Learned engine should be able to surface for any facility operating
heat exchangers or handling hazardous process fluids: mechanical integrity
and management-of-change gaps are recurring themes across the industry, not
one-off events.
