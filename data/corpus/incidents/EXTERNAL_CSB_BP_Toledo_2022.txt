EXTERNAL INDUSTRY REFERENCE — U.S. CHEMICAL SAFETY BOARD (CSB) INVESTIGATION
Source: U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board, csb.gov
Facility: BP-Husky Toledo Refinery, Oregon, Ohio, USA
Incident date: 20 September 2022

SUMMARY
An overflow of the Absorber Stripper Tower released liquid to the fuel gas
system at the BP Toledo Refinery. The facility's process hazard analyses
(PHAs) had identified furnace safety instrumented systems (SIS) and
emergency pressure-relief valves as the safeguards against this overflow
scenario, but the CSB found that neither safeguard was effective in
preventing the liquid overflow on the day of the incident.

RELEVANCE — SAFEGUARD VERIFICATION
This is a documented, public example of a common industrial failure pattern:
a safeguard (relief valve or safety instrumented system) that is *listed* in
a process hazard analysis as protecting against a scenario, but whose actual
functional readiness was not independently verified before it was needed. It
underscores why a compliance and RCA system should track not just whether a
protective device exists on paper, but its actual inspection and test history
against the interval its listed protection assumes — exactly the gap the
compliance agent's hybrid rule-check (AI parses the requirement, code
verifies the record) is designed to catch before a real demand occurs.
